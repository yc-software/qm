# Finance interpretation and output

Lead with the conclusion, scope, cutoff, currency basis, accounting book, and whether the period is open or closed. Separate source facts, calculations produced from accepted evidence, and interpretation.

Quantify drivers in both amount and percentage where meaningful. Separate recurring activity, timing, one-time items, reclassifications, currency effects, migration effects, and data limitations. Do not invent a driver when transaction-level evidence does not support one.

For aging, state the aging date, due-date fallback, bucket definitions, disputed or blocked populations, credits and unapplied cash treatment, and whether days past due are calendar or contractual days.

For variance analysis, keep actual, budget, forecast, and prior-period populations disjoint. Reconcile their entity, account, currency, and period definitions before comparison.

For open periods, label results preliminary and identify relevant unposted activity, pending approvals, incomplete feeds, reversals, and late postings only when supported by authoritative evidence.

State the materiality policy. Use an engagement threshold when supplied. Otherwise request one or label an explicit analytical threshold as provisional and non-audit.

Every deliverable should include:

- question answered and population definition;
- source evidence identifiers and digests;
- retrieval timestamp and cutoff;
- row/page completeness status;
- material exclusions and unresolved differences;
- control or reconciliation performed;
- concise decision-relevant interpretation;
- no credentials, unnecessary personal data, or raw sensitive fields.
